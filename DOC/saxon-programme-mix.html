How area is drawn. Every block's on-screen area is proportional to its floor area at one constant scale across the whole page, so a 1 350 m² sports hall is exactly ten times the block of a 135 m² pair of classrooms. Blocks are packed by squarified treemap and grouped by category, so a floor's mix reads as contiguous regions. The dashed red line is the plate area net of the circulation allowance; anything crossing it is a real overrun, not a drawing artefact.

Shuffle. Each roll is a fresh arrangement, not noise. What is physically fixed stays fixed — the hall and reception sit at grade every time — and everything else is drawn from weighted preferences, taking the ground floor while it has room and moving up when it does not. So each arrangement is plausible but differently compromised, and a capacity error that survives is real: those plates genuinely cannot hold that programme. The seed beside the button names the current roll; press it to lay the same one out again after you have moved things around.

What it does not know. This is a mix diagram, not a plan — it has no geometry, so it cannot tell you whether a 1 350 m² hall with 9 m clear height actually fits under three storeys, whether a floor plate's proportions can hold the rooms assigned to it, or anything about the site, structure or daylight. Adjacency is tested by floor separation only: same floor passes, one floor apart warns, two or more fails.

Colour. Four categories rather than the brief's five — assembly, dining and care are merged into one shared group, because five hues cannot be told apart under colour-vision deficiency when blocks touch on all sides. The four here were validated for that; red and amber are reserved for errors and warnings and never label a category. Areas are indicative competition values (§20).
